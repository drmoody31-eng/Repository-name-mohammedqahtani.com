Noticing thinner hair or more shedding?
It's common, and it can be treated

Hair thinning and shedding in women are far more common than most people think. In most cases there is a clear cause, and with the right diagnosis and treatment, most women see real improvement.

Why does it happen?

The most common cause is female pattern (hereditary) thinning: the part gets wider and more scalp shows on top, especially under bright light.

Heavy shedding is often triggered 2–3 months after stress, illness, a strict diet or fast weight loss, or childbirth. Low iron or vitamin D, thyroid or hormonal changes can also play a part. Each cause needs a different treatment, so diagnosis comes first.

When should you see a specialist?

  • Shedding has lasted more than 3 months
  • Shedding is heavy and isn't slowing down
  • Your part looks wider, or your scalp shows more under light
  • Your ponytail feels thinner than before, or thinning runs in your family

What happens at the clinic?

1. Scalp examination

We examine your scalp and hair follicles under magnification to find the exact type of hair loss.

2. Targeted tests

We check iron, vitamin D, thyroid and hormones, because a deficiency can keep shedding going.

3. A plan that fits you

Treatment matched to your diagnosis, with in-clinic sessions if needed and regular follow-up.
